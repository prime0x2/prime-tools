Why we stopped shipping on Fridays — and what we do instead

By Sam Rivera · 9 min read

For years our release train left every Friday afternoon. It felt efficient: a week of work, a clean cut, a weekend for the dust to settle. In practice, the dust settled on whoever was on call.

The fix wasn’t a rule. It was making releases boring. Small changes, behind flags, rolled out to one percent of traffic first. When a deploy is that dull, the day of the week stops mattering, and Friday becomes just another afternoon.

We started by measuring how long a change sat between merge and production. The median was four days. The worst case was three weeks, mostly because a big release bundled everything together and nobody wanted to be the one to break it.

Here is what changed, and what it cost us to get there.
